Runs into wins.

A league season of Perfect Team is 162 games, and promotion counts wins, not runs. How closely do wins follow runs, how much is luck, and can a roster do anything about it? Seven complete league seasons, 216 team seasons.

How much luck is normal

Compare a team's record with what its runs scored and allowed predict, and the gap spreads about four wins a season either way. Three times that is very rare but happens: it takes a season where nearly everything close breaks the wrong way.

The standard formula is close, a little generous

The usual Pythagorean exponent of 1.83 slightly overrates strong teams in this game; the best fit across all seasons is a bit lower, around 1.7. Any single 30-team season pins it down only loosely, so one season's odd number is mostly noise.

Good teams don't get much worse luck

Strong teams land slightly under the formula, about one win for a team a run a game better than its league. That's real but small. One season can make it look much bigger; across seven it settles near one win.

The bullpen doesn't steer it

A better late-inning bullpen doesn't turn the same runs into more wins. Hold run differential steady and the high-leverage pen's quality explains nothing about who beats the formula. A pen is worth the runs it saves, no more.

Where a lost season goes

When a strong team badly under-wins its runs, it usually comes from two places:

  • BlowoutsA 12 to 2 win adds ten runs to the differential and one win to the record. A lineup whose runs come in bunches pads its run differential without padding its wins.padding
  • One-run gamesMostly coin flips. A bad run of them can cost several wins, and nothing in the roster that we can find controls it.chance

What it means

Run differential is still the right yardstick for a roster. There's no reliable way to win more close games on purpose. For a promotion race, plan on the formula with about a win off for a strong team, and luck of about four wins either way.
