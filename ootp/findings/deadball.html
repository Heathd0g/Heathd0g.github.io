Deadball, as the game plays it.

Tournaments set in 1920 and earlier play a different game. These notes come from thirteen dead-ball tournament runs, from 1888 settings to 1920, every team's real lines, set beside modern-era runs for scale.

How the game plays

Home runs come at about an eighth of the modern rate, strikeouts at under half. Walks are a little rarer, stolen bases about the same, and batting average on balls in play a touch lower. In the 1919 and 1920 parks, runs still score at roughly the modern rate; in the 1888 to 1908 settings, far less.

A dead-ball game is singles, doubles, walks and baserunning. The ball in play decides it.

  • Home runsabout 2 to 3 per 600 plate appearances, against 18 to 19 in a modern tournament1/8
  • Strikeouts9 to 13% of plate appearances, against 23 to 25% nowunder half
  • Runs3.4 a game a side in 1907, 5.3 in 1920 at Fenwaypark matters

What a rating buys there

The same rating does a different amount of work by era. Compared with modern play:

  • BABIPWorth as much as in any era, and more of the game runs through balls in play. The best thing a dead-ball bat can have.first
  • EyeFull value: walks matter as much as ever.second
  • Gap and SpeedPay in the parks that let doubles through.park
  • Avoid KAbout half its modern value: there are fewer strikeouts to avoid.half
  • PowerA quarter to a fifth of its modern worth. It still shows in some tournaments, never at the size the ratings suggest.1/5

On the mound, Stuff still misses bats and shows up everywhere. Control and pBABIP both matter: with so few strikeouts and homers, the walk and the ball in play are most of a pitcher's job. Suppressing home runs buys almost nothing you can measure.

What hurts most to lack

  • Left-handed batsLefties out-hit righties in four of five dead-ball tournaments, by 20 to 40 points. The exception was Fenway 1919, where righties led.hands
  • Starters who finishHigh-stamina starters throw 120 to 130 pitches and close to nine innings with no measurable cost per inning. The best teams carried more stamina, used four starters, and started lefties more often.stamina
  • A pen for the field's handsDead-ball fields run close to half left-handed bats; the winners' staffs fit that.fit
  • Real base thievesOnly elite stealers come out ahead on the bases. Average ones break even; below that, they cost runs.elite only
  • A center fielderA great glove in center is worth far more in early eras than in modern ones. A light-hitting glove man who wouldn't play now can be the right call here.era

Bunting: teams lay down a bit under one sacrifice a game. Bunts cost a little scoring but not wins, which fits bunts coming where one run matters most. It's no reason to bunt more, and no reason to stop.

What the winners carried

The cards the best quarter of each field played well above the field's rate, where we have enough runs to say:

  • 1920 Fenway, 128 teamsKing Kelly C, Donie Bush SS, Harry Steinfeldt 3B, Shoeless Joe Jackson and Tillie Walker LF; Jack Coombs starting. Righties out-hit lefties here; Gap and Speed paid.meta
