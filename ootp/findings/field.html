How the field builds.

What thousands of tournament teams actually do with their 26: where the value goes between rotation, pen and lineup, how left and right are balanced, and what the bench is for. With the best quarter of each field set beside everyone.

Dead-ball tournaments

  • PitchingAbout a third of the roster's value: a four-man rotation and three or four relievers. The winners carry the fourth reliever more often.1/3
  • Lineup handsRighty-heavy by value, about three to two. In a couple of events (1920 Fenway, 1888 settings) lefty starters out-spend righties.3 : 2
  • The benchA real one: close to four bench bats, about two of them true pinch hitters. With no DH, the pitcher's spot gets hit for, and the field builds for it.deep

The pinch hitters are good bats, not fillers: Willie Keeler, Bobby Wallace, Bob Fothergill and Otto Schomberg show up on dozens of teams each. By the 1940s settings, the shape already looks modern: relief spending matches the rotation and the bench shrinks to two or three.

Drafted dead-ball teams look different: more men play every day, and the bench is half the size. A draft hands out cards in rounds, so its shape is the format's as much as the manager's.

Cap tournaments

  • PitchingClose to half the cap, and the pen costs more than the rotation: about a quarter of the cap on seven relievers, a fifth on five starters.~1/2
  • Lineup handsRighty bats out-spend lefties about nine to five among the starters.9 : 5
  • The benchOne or two men: mostly one spot starter for rest days, maybe a part-time pinch hitter.1-2

Each event has its own shape. The cheaper caps spend most on the pen; the Gold caps split rotation and pen evenly. The 1970s-set caps are different again: equal money on lefty and righty starters, only five relievers, and two or three bench bats. Fewer arms needed, more bats carried.

The 1888-set cap is the opposite of a modern one. Pitching takes under 30%, seven everyday bats carry over 40% of the cap, and the staff is three starters and three relievers.

Slots tournaments

The same shape as cap: a quarter of the value in the pen, a fifth in the rotation, righty starters out-spending lefties about two to one, and a bench of one or two. Slot teams platoon a little more than cap teams.

Where the premium slots go: bats first, the rotation second, and the pen gets the lowest tiers in every format. In a slots event with twelve Diamond slots, seven go to hitters, four to the rotation and one or two to the pen. Where a format forces Iron cards, they land in the pen and on the bench.

The bench, everywhere

  • Spot starterThe one bench man every format keeps: about one a team, the rest-day fill. He's the backup the game actually uses.keep
  • Pinch hitterA dead-ball thing: two a team there, under half a card a team in modern cap and slots.era
  • Pure gloveRare: one team in ten in dead-ball, one in twenty in cap. Usually a minimum-value card filling a slot, or a backup catcher who comes in late.rare
  • Pinch runnerEssentially never. The game's manager doesn't run for anyone often enough to show, so a speed-only card is a wasted spot.none
  • Two-way cardsRare too, and the field doesn't build around them.rare
