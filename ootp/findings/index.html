What the numbers say.

Short notes from studying thousands of Perfect Team tournament teams: what actually turns ratings into wins.

A sample, not the gospelSome of these may be wrong, and some may be obvious. They're a sample of the measurements we take, and they'll change as more games come in.

Power creep is real, and steady

At the same card value, newer cards are simply better. A 100-value bat from launch week projects like an 80 to 84 released this fall, and every value band climbs the same way, month after month. Pitchers too. In a cap tournament, an old card's price buys less than it used to.

Old cards still play to their ratings

There's no hidden age penalty: an early card performs about as its ratings say, the same as a new one. The creep is in the ratings themselves, so read the ratings and the release date tells you nothing extra.

Eras change what a hit is worth

In a dead-ball tournament, a home run is worth more runs than in a modern one, because runs are scarce. But homers almost never happen there, so contact, gap power and speed carry the offense. In every era, a homer is still worth far more than a double.

The bullpen is worth its runs, not more

A better late-inning bullpen saves the runs it saves, and that's about it. It doesn't win extra close games beyond that, in tournaments or leagues. Build the pen for run prevention, not for drama.

Pick a home park for your lineup

A park that boosts left-handed hitters can be worth ten runs or more a season to a lefty-heavy league lineup, against a neutral one. Check your lineup's handedness before you pick.
